# KP Liberation Roadmap

This tracks the planned feature arc across upcoming major versions. Detailed design briefs for individual features live in `docs/plans/`; this file is the higher-level sequencing across versions. Version-to-feature allocation below is a **proposed grouping**, not locked — rearrange freely as priorities shift.

## Project Principles (Iron Rules)

These apply across every version on this roadmap, not just the current one. Any feature that violates one of these needs a deliberate, stated exception — not a silent drift. Weigh every future feature against these before it goes into a `/spec` plan.

1. **Realism in flavour, not admin.** Most of the crew is ex- or current-military and values authenticity, but this is still a game played for fun. Mechanics should feel grounded without turning into tedious bookkeeping or checklist management — if a "realistic" addition creates busywork without creating a meaningful decision, cut it.
2. **Reward staying in the fight, not ending it early.** The "spawn, do the mission, suicide to respawn faster" loop is explicitly the thing to design against. Every system should give players a real, felt reason to survive the whole engagement, bring their vehicle and gear home, and re-up for the next push — not just as an end-of-campaign payoff, but session to session and within a single mission too. This is the throughline behind the vehicle-ownership pillar, progression's cumulative (never-reset) scoring, and the convoy plan's route-preservation fix — three separate features that all exist because something in the current game currently punishes or ignores playing carefully.
3. **AI fills the gaps, it doesn't play the game for you.** A 3–4 (rarely 5–6) person crew makes heavy AI reliance necessary, but a feature that lets players just watch an outcome resolve itself removes the reason to be there. Where a fight or decision is the actual point (a firefight, defending a convoy, holding a sector under pressure), AI should support or be summonable, not silently substitute for player action. Where a task is pure labour (driving a resupply route, crewing a taxi's flight, holding already-secured ground), AI should do it outright. This is why QRF/garrison exist to help take and hold ground rather than take it *for* the squad unsupervised, and why convoy escort became "the convoy calls the players" rather than "players call in an AI stand-in." Make this opt-in/opt-out per feature where practical, not uniform across everyone.
4. **No mechanic requires more bodies than the crew has.** If a feature needs a dedicated player in a pure support role (a standing pilot, a full-time driver, a second commander) to function at all, it needs an AI-assisted path as a first-class option, not an afterthought — the deciding factor behind every AI-crewed system speced this session (taxi, QRF, garrison, convoy trucks).
5. **Nothing is free.** Every convenience, power, or safety net a system hands the squad should carry a proportional cost or real risk — the taxi's fuel cost and threat-abort logic, QRF needing its own balance pass rather than inheriting the taxi's numbers, and convoy risk being decoupled from a single reputation gate are all instances of this. A zero-consequence button anywhere quietly recreates the exact snowball problem this whole roadmap exists to fix.
6. **Persistence and consequence over reset** — the general principle behind rule 2, extended past vehicles: a player's rank, a convoy's route, a recruit's service record — anything representing accumulated effort should carry forward wherever technically feasible, rather than silently resetting. Treat an unavoidable reset (like the current per-save vehicle-condition wipe) as a bug to fix, not a fact of life to design around forever.
7. **Reuse existing systems before building parallel ones, and keep each version tightly scoped.** Every plan from this session deliberately reused an existing hook (the taxi's call-in pattern, `KPLIB_owner`, `asymm_notifications`, `empty_vehicles_marker.sqf`) instead of inventing a duplicate mechanism, and the roadmap itself was split into three versions specifically so no single release absorbs everything. A codebase full of near-duplicate systems and a roadmap of bloated versions are the same failure mode from two different angles — keep guarding against both.
8. **Don't half-arse it — ask questions and make suggestions.** A feature write-up needs real substance (grounded research, proper acceptance criteria, no placeholder fluff), not a shallow pass. Surface open questions, gaps, or better alternatives as they're found rather than silently proceeding on an assumption. Every plan on this roadmap exists because of rounds of questions and suggestions, not a single first draft — keep working that way.

## v0.97.0 — Assets Have Value, Small Crews Get Support

Theme: address the late-campaign "buy it, throw it away, repeat" snowball, and give a small (3–4, rarely 5–6 player) crew AI-supplemented ways to cover both logistics and combat roles. Fully speced:

- **AI helicopter rappel taxi** — AI-crewed insertion/extraction via ACE fast-roping, no player pilot required. (`docs/plans/2026-09-16-ai-heli-rappel-taxi.md`)
- **Player progression / veterancy** — custom per-campaign rank track from combat, logi, and support actions; cosmetic + personal AI-recruit-quality rewards; penalties for civilian/team kills and vehicle abandonment. (`docs/plans/2026-09-16-player-progression.md`)
- **Logistics convoy visibility & distress-call escort** — convoys become real, always-visible AI-driven trucks that call for player help when threatened, fixing the existing bug where a defended convoy's route was wiped anyway. (`docs/plans/2026-09-16-logi-convoy-visibility-escort.md`)
- **Fortified town strongpoints** — large and medium town sectors concentrate a few defenders into 1-3 densely garrisoned, road-facing buildings instead of scattering one random-facing soldier per building, and can't be captured while a strongpoint still holds. Pulled forward from its original v0.98.0 "Take & Hold" grouping — it has no dependency on either item there. (`docs/plans/2026-09-17-fortified-town-strongpoints.md`)

Raw, not-yet-design-reviewed notes for later versions were captured in `docs/plans/2026-09-16-v097-backlog-notes.md` and are sorted into v0.98/v0.99 below.

**Open loose end:** the taxi was designed as the "honest" alternative to HALO's instant-teleport-with-a-parachute feel, but nobody has decided whether HALO itself changes as a result — stays exactly as-is as a separate fast-travel option, gets its cooldown/positioning adjusted now that a "real" insertion method exists alongside it, or is retired. Needs an explicit decision before v0.97.0 ships, not an implicit one.

## v0.98.0 — Take & Hold

Theme: extend the AI-crewed call-in pattern proven by the v0.97.0 taxi from *transport* into *combat support*, closing the loop on sector assaults so a small crew can both take and hold ground without needing extra bodies.

- **AI QRF (armed reinforcement call-in)** — call in an armed helicopter full of AI friendlies to help capture a sector, for a resource cost. Must auto-exfil once the sector clears or turns friendly — no idle AI left behind. Directly reuses the taxi's AI-crewed call-in/flight pattern. **Before design work starts, check for overlap with the existing `reinforcements_manager.sqf` system** — not yet verified whether this would duplicate an existing mechanic.
  - **Do not reuse the taxi's cost/cooldown numbers.** QRF is strictly more powerful than the taxi — armed AI that fights *for* the squad, not just a ride — so it needs its own balance pass or it risks trivialising sector captures.
  - **Must respect (or explicitly bypass) the existing server-wide unit cap** — `KPLIB_blufor_cap`/`unit_cap.sqf` already caps total BLUFOR units; QRF's spawned AI friendlies aren't accounted for against that anywhere yet.
  - **Must decide whether QRF kills feed the v0.97.0 progression system's per-player kill points.** If they do, this is a farming exploit (call in AI, let it get kills, collect points for free) unless deliberately mitigated; if they don't, that exclusion should be a stated design decision, not an oversight.
- **AI sector garrison** — assign recruits to hold a captured sector/FOB after the fight, so players don't have to personally camp every objective. Pairs directly with QRF: QRF takes a sector, garrison holds it afterward. The v0.97.0 fortified-strongpoints feature already records which buildings were a sector's strongpoints in `KPLIB_sector_strongpoints`, so garrison can read where to hold instead of inventing its own "where do I stand" concept from scratch.

Both items still need the same multi-round research-and-design-fork treatment the three v0.97.0 features went through before they're `/spec`-ready — currently they're one-paragraph notes, not design briefs.

## v0.99.0 — Assets That Matter, Combat With Texture

Theme: the biggest deferred pillar from the whole planning arc — vehicles and assets carrying real, persistent value — plus two lighter refinements that give combat more shape than a single global difficulty dial.

- **Vehicle value & ownership** — the root fix behind the entire "assets have no value" problem this whole roadmap started from. Persist vehicle condition (damage/fuel/loadout/cargo) across saves, let players name/claim vehicles with a visible history/kill log, rework the flat 50% recycle refund to reward upkeep over scrap-and-rebuy cycling, add a wear/maintenance sink restored via FOB supply, support salvage-and-repair as an alternative to scrapping, and tie vehicle condition to actual performance. Builds on the `KPLIB_owner` tag already introduced by the v0.97.0 progression plan rather than inventing a second ownership mechanism. **This is a save-format change** — the riskiest, most invasive item on the whole roadmap, which is why it gets a version of its own instead of competing for attention alongside other features.
  - **Sequencing question worth revisiting**: three earlier plans already carry a "known gap" footnote because this hasn't shipped yet — the taxi explicitly accepts the persistence gap for v1, the progression plan's `KPLIB_owner` tag doesn't survive a save, and the convoy plan defers all truck persistence "for now." Rather than accumulating a fourth version of debt, it may be worth pulling just the **underlying save-format persistence capability** (not the full naming/history/recycle-curve UX) forward into v0.98.0, so later features can build on real persistence instead of each working around its absence. Not decided — flagging for whoever scopes v0.98.0/v0.99.0 in `/spec` to weigh.
- **Named/high-value enemy targets** — elite or named enemy leaders as discrete kill objectives with a distinct payoff (intel, a hit to that region's combat readiness), feeding directly into the v0.97.0 progression system's kill-based points as a bonus-value target instead of flat per-kill grinding.
- **AI recruit veterancy** — recruits accumulate their own service history/experience and become worth protecting, the same "personnel is an asset" logic as vehicle ownership applied to people. **Distinct from** the v0.97.0 progression plan's recruit-quality-by-player-rank reward (that scales a recruit's quality based on the commanding *player's* rank; this would give the *recruit itself* independent, persistent history regardless of who commands it). Explicitly deferred out of the progression plan's scope — captured here so it isn't lost.
- **Regional/localised combat readiness** — replace or supplement the single global `combat_readiness` dial with something tied to *where* player aggression actually happens, adding tactical texture as a refinement of an existing system rather than a new one. **Correction from initial framing**: this was originally described as a "lightweight companion" item, which undersells it — a closer look shows it would touch `readiness_increase.sqf`, `counter_battlegroup.sqf`, `random_battlegroups.sqf`, and reinforcement timing, i.e. most of the AI escalation logic. Scope this as a real rebalance in its own right when it reaches `/spec`, not a quick add-on.

None of the v0.99.0 items have been through the research-and-design-fork discussion the v0.97.0 features got either — treat this section as a themed grouping of raw ideas, same caveat as v0.98.0.

## Cross-Cutting Considerations

Concerns that span multiple versions/features rather than belonging to one:

- **"Someone needs you" notification fatigue.** By v0.98.0, a 3–4 person crew could be juggling convoy distress calls (up to 2 concurrent, v0.97.0), QRF requests (v0.98.0), and existing secondary-objective alerts, all competing for attention with no shared priority or triage UI. None of the individual plans address this because it only appears once they're combined — worth a dedicated design pass on a unified "requests/threats" surface before all three ship independently and collide in practice.
- **Persistence sequencing** — see the sequencing question under v0.99.0's vehicle value & ownership entry above; it affects how three already-speced v0.97.0 features (taxi, progression, convoy) eventually close their own "known gap" footnotes.
